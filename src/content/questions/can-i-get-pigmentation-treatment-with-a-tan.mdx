Having a recent tan is one of the most common reasons a pigmentation
treatment appointment gets delayed or adjusted, and this is a genuinely
important safety consideration rather than an arbitrary rule.

## Why a tan changes things

Light and laser-based pigmentation treatments, including BBL, work partly
by targeting melanin in the skin. When skin is tanned, there is more
melanin spread across the whole treated area, not just in the pigmentation
itself, which makes it harder for a treatment to target dark spots
accurately and increases the risk of unwanted pigment changes, including
lightening or further darkening of the surrounding skin.

## What this means in a place like Cairns

Cairns' high year-round UV index means many people carry some degree of
tan for most of the year, even without deliberately sun-baking, simply
from regular outdoor exposure. This makes tan assessment a routine,
ongoing part of pigmentation treatment planning here, rather than a
one-off, seasonal consideration.

## What generally happens instead

Depending on how recent and how developed the tan is, a clinic may
recommend waiting for it to fade before treatment, adjusting the treatment
settings, or in some cases advising against a particular treatment
altogether for that session. This is assessed individually, since skin
type, tan depth and the specific treatment involved all affect the level
of risk.

## What you can do

Maintaining strict sun protection in the weeks before a booked
pigmentation treatment, rather than only from the appointment onward,
gives the clinic a clearer, safer picture of your skin to work with, and
reduces the likelihood of needing to reschedule.

## The bottom line

Suitability with a tan is not something to guess at home. A consultation
is the appropriate way to find out whether treatment can proceed as
planned, needs adjusting, or should be rescheduled for your specific skin.
