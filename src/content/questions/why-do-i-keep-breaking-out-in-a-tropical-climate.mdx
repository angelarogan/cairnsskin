Heat and humidity change the skin's environment in ways that make breakouts
more likely and, for some people, more stubborn than the breakouts they might
be used to in a drier or cooler climate.

## Why humidity affects breakouts

Higher temperatures increase oil (sebum) production, and humidity slows how
quickly sweat evaporates from the skin. The combination of sweat, oil, dead
skin cells and everyday grime creates a sticky layer that can block pores more
easily. This is why breakouts in a tropical climate are often more persistent
and can flare up faster after exercise, humid weather or heavy sunscreen use.

## Acne or fungal acne?

Not every breakout in a hot, humid climate is typical acne. Malassezia yeast,
which lives naturally on everyone's skin, can multiply more readily in warm,
humid conditions and cause a condition often called fungal acne, technically
Malassezia folliculitis. It tends to look like small, uniform, itchy bumps
rather than the mixed blackheads, whiteheads and larger inflamed spots typical
of ordinary acne, and it does not respond to standard acne treatments in the
same way, since it is not caused by the same bacteria.

## What tends to help

For typical acne, lightweight, non-comedogenic (oil-free) skincare and
sunscreen, regular cleansing after sweating, and avoiding heavy occlusive
products can all help. If breakouts look uniform, itchy and unresponsive to
usual acne products, that pattern is worth mentioning specifically in a
consultation, since fungal acne needs a different approach and treating it as
ordinary acne can make it linger longer than necessary.
