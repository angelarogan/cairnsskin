## What actually matters in an acne face wash

The two ingredients worth checking a label for are salicylic acid and benzoyl peroxide. Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, so it can get into the pore lining and help loosen the build-up that turns into blackheads and whiteheads, while benzoyl peroxide works mainly by reducing the surface bacteria linked to inflamed, red pimples. Clinical guidance notes that salicylic acid softens and descales the skin to reduce comedones, while benzoyl peroxide is available in strengths from 2.5% up to 10%, with the lower end sufficient for most people and higher strengths carrying more irritation risk.

## Why a cleanser has real limits

A face wash only sits on the skin for a matter of seconds before being rinsed off, which is very different to a leave-on gel or lotion with the same active ingredient. Clinical guidance actually points out that an unmedicated wash is often enough as a base step, and that a rinse-off cleanser is the gentler option if a leave-on product is causing irritation. That's a useful reframe: a wash is mainly there to clear away oil, sweat and debris without stripping the skin, not to be the primary treatment doing the heavy lifting on active acne.

## Questions worth asking before you buy anything

Rather than chasing the highest percentage on the shelf, it's more useful to ask what concentration is actually appropriate for your skin type and how inflamed your acne currently is, whether the formulation is a gentle gel or foam suited to daily use, and whether you're layering the same active ingredient across multiple steps of a routine, which can dry out and irritate skin rather than help it. Rough scrubbing or over-cleansing tends to make inflammatory acne worse, not better, regardless of what's in the bottle.

## When a professional opinion actually helps

In Cairns' heat and humidity, oil production and sweat can make breakouts feel constant no matter which cleanser is used, and persistent, hormonal or cystic acne patterns generally need more than a shelf product to shift. It's also worth setting realistic timeframes: milder breakouts can start settling within a few weeks of a properly matched routine, while more stubborn or cystic acne is more often a months-long process regardless of the approach taken. A professional skin consultation can properly assess your acne type and severity, and map out whether that means adjusting your home routine, adding an in-clinic option such as a professional peel or light-based treatment, or a combination of both, rather than working through pharmacy shelves by trial and error.
