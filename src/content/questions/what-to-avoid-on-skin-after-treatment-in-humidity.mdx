Aftercare advice for skin treatments is fairly consistent across Australia,
but Cairns' heat and humidity make some parts of it more important, and
easier to slip up on, than they would be in a cooler climate.

## Sun exposure

Skin is often more sensitive to UV directly after treatments such as BBL,
skin needling or peels. Cairns' high year-round UV index means unprotected
exposure, even briefly, carries more risk of pigmentation or irritation
during the healing window than it would somewhere with lower UV. Strict
sun avoidance and SPF 50+ reapplication matter more here, not less, in the
days after treatment.

## Heat and sweating

Heat and humidity increase sweating, and sweat can irritate freshly treated
skin or increase the risk of infection around areas with micro-injuries, such
as after skin needling. Avoiding strenuous exercise, saunas, hot showers and
swimming for the period recommended by the clinic is generally more relevant
in Cairns simply because opportunities to overheat or sweat heavily are more
constant.

## Active skincare ingredients

Retinoids, exfoliating acids and other active ingredients are commonly
paused for a period after treatment, since freshly treated skin is more
prone to irritation. Combined with heat and humidity, irritated skin can
also become a fungal acne risk, so a simple, gentle skincare routine tends to
work better than reintroducing actives too early.

## The general rule

Aftercare instructions vary by treatment and by individual skin, so the
specific guidance given by whoever performed the treatment should always
take priority over general advice. If in doubt about timing or a reaction
that doesn't seem right, checking in with the clinic directly is the safest
approach.
