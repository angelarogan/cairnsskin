## What's actually in this serum

Rather than building around a single active the way many basic acne products do, Skinstitut's AHA + BHA Clearing Serum layers a few different ingredient types together: salicylic acid (a beta hydroxy acid), lactic and mandelic acid (alpha hydroxy acids), niacinamide, and bakuchiol, alongside hydrating ingredients like sodium hyaluronate and glycerin to help offset how drying acids can be on their own.

## How each ingredient actually works

Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, so it can get down into the pore lining itself and help break up the mix of oil and dead skin cells that turns into blackheads, whiteheads and general congestion. Lactic and mandelic acid work differently, resurfacing the skin's surface layer to smooth texture and support cell turnover, with mandelic acid in particular considered gentler and generally better tolerated on sensitive or acne-prone skin than some other AHAs. Niacinamide helps calm inflammation and supports the skin barrier, which matters because acids on their own can leave skin reactive if nothing is helping it recover. Bakuchiol is commonly used as a gentler alternative to retinoids, offering some similar cell-turnover benefits without the same level of initial irritation.

## Why formulation matters as much as the ingredient list

Combining an oil-soluble BHA with two AHAs in one serum only works well if the concentrations are balanced enough not to over-exfoliate the skin, since layering several acids without any buffering can leave the skin barrier compromised rather than genuinely clearer. This is part of why a properly formulated serum differs meaningfully from mixing several single-ingredient products together at home: formulation and concentration are doing real work, not just the ingredient names on the label.

## What a serum like this can and can't do

A well-formulated acid serum can genuinely help with congestion, texture and mild breakouts, and consistent use over several weeks is generally where people start noticing a difference, with the brand's own testing on this serum citing visible improvement at around four weeks, though individual results vary. It isn't a substitute for daily SPF, since exfoliating acids increase sun sensitivity, and it isn't likely to be enough on its own for hormonal, cystic or scarring acne, which usually needs a broader plan. If breakouts are persistent, painful or leaving marks behind, a professional skin consultation can assess what's actually driving them and whether a serum like this fits into a bigger plan, or isn't the right starting point at all.
