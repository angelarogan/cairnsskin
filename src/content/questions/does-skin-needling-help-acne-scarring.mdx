Skin needling, also called collagen induction therapy, uses fine needles to
create controlled micro-injuries in the skin. The body responds by producing
new collagen and elastin as part of normal wound healing, which is the
mechanism behind any improvement in scar appearance.

## What it can help with

Skin needling is most often discussed for atrophic (indented) acne scarring,
where collagen remodelling can soften the edges of a scar and make the
overall skin texture look smoother over time. Improvement tends to build
gradually across a course of sessions rather than appearing after a single
treatment, and it is generally described as a softening or smoothing effect
rather than complete removal of scarring.

## Where it falls short

Not all acne scarring responds equally well. Deep, narrow "ice pick" scars
and raised (hypertrophic or keloid) scars typically respond less predictably
to needling alone and may need a different or combined approach. Outcomes
also depend on skin type, scar depth and how consistently a treatment course
is followed, so results are not guaranteed and can vary noticeably between
individuals.

## What to expect around treatment

Some redness and sensitivity for a few days afterwards is common, and
sun protection matters more than usual while skin is healing. Because
outcomes depend heavily on the specific type of scarring involved, an
in-person assessment is the only reliable way to know whether skin needling
is a sensible option for your scarring, or whether another approach would
suit it better.
