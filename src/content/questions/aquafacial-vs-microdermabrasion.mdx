AquaFacial and microdermabrasion are both gentle, low-downtime treatments
that exfoliate the skin's surface, and they're often assumed to do the same
thing. The main difference comes down to how each removes dead skin and
what happens afterwards: one adds moisture back in as it exfoliates, the
other is a dry mechanical process generally built around a longer course of
sessions.

## How AquaFacial works

AquaFacial is a multi-step, water-based treatment. A handheld device
combines gentle exfoliation with light vacuum suction to lift built-up
oil, dead skin and debris out of the pores, then finishes by infusing
hydrating serums, such as hyaluronic acid, into the skin's surface. Because
the process is wet rather than dry, and ends with hydration rather than
exfoliation alone, most people notice an immediately plumper, brighter
look straight after a session. See
[AquaFacial in Cairns](/treatments/aquafacial/) for more on what a session
involves.

## How microdermabrasion works

Microdermabrasion is a dry, mechanical exfoliation treatment. A device
buffs away the outermost layer of dead skin cells to smooth texture and
reduce the appearance of congestion. There's no hydrating infusion step
built in, and results build cumulatively: most people need a course of
multiple sessions, often at fortnightly or monthly intervals, to see and
maintain an improvement in overall texture. See
[Microdermabrasion in Cairns](/treatments/microdermabrasion/) for more on
what to expect.

## Where they differ

The clearest difference is what each treatment adds back to the skin.
AquaFacial finishes with a hydrating serum infusion, so its immediate
effect leans toward plumpness and glow. Microdermabrasion is purely
exfoliating, so its immediate effect leans toward smoother, refined
texture without an added hydration boost. AquaFacial's extraction step
also specifically targets congested pores and blackheads, which isn't
part of a standard microdermabrasion session. Microdermabrasion is
generally considered a lighter, more mechanical treatment that's often
built into an ongoing maintenance course, while AquaFacial is commonly
chosen as more of a standalone glow treatment, though both can be repeated
regularly.

## Which one might suit your skin

If your main concern is dehydration, dullness or congested, blocked
pores, the combined extraction and hydration in an AquaFacial is
generally the closer fit. If your main concern is rough, uneven surface
texture, or you're after a lighter maintenance treatment as part of a
regular routine, microdermabrasion is generally the closer fit. Neither
treatment reaches deeper concerns such as established acne scarring, fine
lines or significant pigmentation, since both work at the surface layer of
skin only. For a closer look at AquaFacial's specific limits, see
[what are the real benefits of an AquaFacial?](/questions/what-are-the-real-benefits-of-an-aquafacial/)

## Getting it right

Because skin type, oiliness and sensitivity all affect which treatment
feels more comfortable and delivers a better result, a professional skin
consultation is the most reliable way to work out which of the two suits
your skin, or whether a different treatment altogether would better
address what you're trying to fix.
