Any injury to the skin, including a mosquito bite, a scratch from heat rash,
or a popped pimple, can trigger a mark called post-inflammatory
hyperpigmentation (PIH) once it heals. This is not a scar in the usual
sense, it is extra melanin the skin produces in response to inflammation,
and it is one of the most common causes of new dark spots reported in
Cairns' warmer, more humid months.

## Why this happens more in a tropical climate

Cairns has a high year-round mosquito population, and heat rash from
sweating in humid conditions is common, particularly through the wet
season. Both cause inflammation in the skin. Darker or more melanin-rich
skin types tend to produce more pigment in response to this inflammation,
and Cairns' intense UV exposure adds to it, since sun exposure on
already-inflamed or healing skin can make the resulting mark darker and
slower to fade than it would in a cooler, lower-UV climate.

## Scratching makes it worse

Scratching a bite or heat rash extends the inflammation and increases the
risk of a lasting mark, and can also introduce bacteria that leads to
further irritation, both of which prolong pigmentation. Resisting the urge
to scratch, and keeping bites and irritated skin protected from further
friction, genuinely reduces how dark and how long-lasting the mark
becomes.

## What helps it fade

Daily broad-spectrum SPF 50+ over the healing area limits further
darkening from UV exposure while the mark fades. Most post-inflammatory
marks fade on their own over weeks to months, though this can take longer
in a high-UV climate without consistent sun protection. For marks that
persist well beyond this, in-clinic pigmentation treatments are sometimes
considered, though a consultation is needed to assess whether this is
appropriate for the type and depth of the mark.

## When to see a doctor instead

A mark that is changing shape, growing, becoming raised, or not settling
into a normal healing pattern should be checked by a doctor rather than
assumed to be simple pigmentation.
