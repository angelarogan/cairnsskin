## Medical skin care versus cosmetic skin care

Cairns has a mix of skin cancer and dermatology clinics on one hand, and cosmetic or aesthetic skin clinics on the other, and it is worth knowing which one you actually need before you book. If you have a new or changing mole, a lesion that won't heal, or a family history of skin cancer, that is a job for a GP skin check, a skin cancer clinic or a dermatologist, not a cosmetic appointment. If your concern is pigmentation, acne, redness, texture or early ageing, that is where a cosmetic skin clinic with appropriately qualified staff comes in. Living in Far North Queensland, most people end up needing both at different points, given the region's UV exposure and humidity.

## What actually matters when choosing where to go

Qualifications matter more than décor, and different treatments call for different checks. For cosmetic injectables specifically, such as anti-wrinkle injections or dermal fillers, the practitioner needs to be an AHPRA-registered health professional: doctors can prescribe and perform the full range, and registered nurses can inject under a doctor's oversight. You can check anyone's standing on the public AHPRA register.

General skin treatments, facials, peels, skin needling and similar services, sit outside AHPRA's scope. There, it is worth checking the therapist holds a genuine qualification such as a Diploma of Beauty Therapy, rather than assuming any staff member is equally trained. If a clinic is offering treatment with a Class 4 laser, that is a separate requirement again: in Queensland, the individual operator needs to hold a current Cosmetic Laser Use Licence issued by Queensland Health under the Radiation Safety Act 1999, and the business itself needs a Cosmetic Possession Licence covering that equipment. A reputable clinic should be upfront about which of these applies to your specific treatment and happy to show it, and should never promise guaranteed or permanent results, since no legitimate provider can honestly make that claim for skin treatments.

## Questions worth asking before you book

A genuine consultation should come before any treatment is booked in, not after. That consultation is the point where a practitioner actually looks at your skin, asks about your history, sun exposure and any reactions you have had before, and explains realistic outcomes and limitations, rather than pushing a package. It is reasonable to ask what device or technique is being used and why it suits your skin type, how many sessions are realistically needed, what the known side effects are, and what happens if your skin does not respond the way you hoped. If those questions get rushed or brushed off, that is a sign to look elsewhere.

## Why this matters more than a shortlist of products

It is tempting to skip the consultation altogether and just buy a serum or device online for pigmentation, redness or texture concerns. Shop-bought products can play a supporting role, but they cannot assess your skin, diagnose what is actually causing a concern, or adjust a plan if something is not working the way an in-person consultation can. If you have not had a professional look at your skin yet, that conversation is generally a more useful starting point than guessing at ingredients on a shelf.
