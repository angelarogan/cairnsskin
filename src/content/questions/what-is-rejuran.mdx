Rejuran is an injectable treatment based on polynucleotides, fragments of DNA
that are used to support the skin's own repair processes rather than to add
volume or directly resurface the skin. It is generally positioned as a skin
quality treatment rather than an anti-wrinkle or scar-removal treatment.

## How it is commonly used

Rejuran is typically injected into the skin in a series of sessions, aimed at
improving overall skin quality, including hydration, elasticity and fine
texture. It is often discussed alongside other skin-quality treatments as an
option for people whose main concern is overall texture and dullness rather
than a specific line, scar or volume loss.

## What the evidence says

Polynucleotide treatments are a relatively newer category compared to
long-established options such as sunscreen or basic laser treatments, so
independent, large-scale research is still developing. Much of the
information available about specific outcomes and timelines comes from
manufacturer or brand material, which should be read as commercial
information rather than independent clinical evidence. This does not mean
the treatment doesn't work, but it does mean claims about specific results
should be treated cautiously until backed by independent research.

## Suitability and limitations

Rejuran is not a substitute for sun protection, and it is not generally
positioned as a treatment for deep acne scarring or significant volume loss,
where other options may be more appropriate. As with any injectable
treatment, individual suitability depends on skin condition, medical history
and expectations, which is why this is genuinely a consultation-first
treatment rather than one to self-select based on marketing claims.
