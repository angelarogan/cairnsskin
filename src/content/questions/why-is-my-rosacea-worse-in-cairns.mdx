Rosacea is a chronic condition that causes facial redness, visible blood
vessels and, for some people, small bumps or sensitivity, and it is well known
for flaring in response to specific triggers rather than appearing constantly
at the same intensity.

## Why heat and humidity are common triggers

Heat causes blood vessels in the skin to dilate, which is part of why rosacea
often flares during exercise, hot showers or hot weather. Humidity adds to
this by trapping heat against the skin and slowing evaporative cooling, so the
skin stays warmer for longer. UV exposure is a separate, well-documented
rosacea trigger, and Cairns' consistently high UV index means sun exposure is
harder to avoid year-round than in a temperate climate where UV drops off in
winter.

## Why it can feel constant here

In many parts of Australia, rosacea sufferers get some seasonal relief over
cooler months. In Cairns, heat, humidity and UV exposure are all present for
most of the year, so the usual pattern of a bad summer followed by an easier
winter is less pronounced, which is why flares can feel more frequent or more
constant.

## What tends to help

Daily broad-spectrum SPF, staying in shade or air-conditioning during peak
heat, lukewarm rather than hot showers, and identifying personal triggers
(which can include spicy food, alcohol or particular skincare ingredients)
are common first steps. Rosacea varies significantly between individuals, so
what triggers a flare for one person may not affect another, and a
consultation is the most reliable way to build a management plan suited to
your specific pattern of flares.
