Laser hair removal works by targeting the pigment (melanin) in hair
follicles with light energy. This is also why a recent tan changes the
safety picture, since tanned skin contains more melanin than usual, closer
to the surface.

## Why a tan increases risk

When skin has more melanin from a tan, laser energy can be absorbed by the
surrounding skin as well as the hair follicle, which increases the risk of
burns, blistering and post-treatment pigmentation changes, including patches
that are either darker or lighter than the surrounding skin. This risk
applies to both natural sun tans and some self-tanning products.

## Why this matters more in Cairns

Cairns' consistently high UV index means it is easy to have some degree of
tan or recent sun exposure for most of the year, even without deliberately
sun-tanning. Because of this, clinics generally ask about recent sun
exposure and may need to delay treatment, or wait for a tan to fade, before
laser hair removal can proceed safely, more often than would come up in a
lower-UV climate.

## What tends to happen in practice

Most clinics assess skin tone and recent sun exposure at each appointment,
not just at the first consultation, since a tan can change between sessions.
Strict sun protection before and after treatment is generally recommended
regardless of skin tone, both to protect the treated area and to keep skin
in a safe, more predictable state for ongoing sessions. If you are unsure
whether recent sun exposure affects your suitability for treatment, this is
worth raising directly at a consultation rather than guessing.
