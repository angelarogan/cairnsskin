Pigmentation, in the most literal sense, just means colour, and everyone's
skin has pigmentation from natural melanin. In everyday skincare
conversation, though, "pigmentation" almost always refers to a specific
concern: patches or spots that look darker than the surrounding skin.

## What causes it

Melanin is produced by cells called melanocytes, and certain triggers make
them produce more of it in a specific area, creating a visibly darker
patch. UV exposure is the most common trigger, which is why sun spots and
freckling are the most familiar form. Hormonal changes (as in melasma) and
inflammation from acne, injury or irritation can also trigger pigmentation
in specific areas.

## Different things people mean by "pigmentation"

The word gets used loosely to cover several distinct patterns: sun spots
and freckling from cumulative UV exposure, melasma from hormonal triggers,
and post-inflammatory marks left behind after a breakout, bite or
irritation heals. These can look similar but behave differently and
respond to different approaches, which is why an accurate description of
what you're actually seeing matters more than the single word
"pigmentation" on its own.

## When it's worth checking with a doctor

Ordinary pigmentation is a cosmetic concern, not a medical one, but any
spot or patch that changes shape, colour or size, becomes raised, itchy or
bleeds should be assessed by a doctor rather than treated as routine
pigmentation, particularly in a high-UV location like Cairns.
