## Two different types of skin care

A dermatologist or skin cancer doctor and a cosmetic skin clinic both deal with skin, but they exist for different reasons. A dermatology or skin cancer practice is focused on diagnosing and treating medical conditions: melanoma and other skin cancers, mole checks, eczema, psoriasis, and other conditions that need a medical assessment, sometimes a biopsy, and occasionally prescription or surgical treatment. A cosmetic skin clinic is generally focused on appearance-related concerns such as pigmentation, fine lines, texture, redness or enlarged pores, using treatments like light-based devices, skin needling or professional-strength peels rather than medical intervention.

In a tropical, high-UV place like Cairns, both types of care matter, and a lot of people end up needing input from both at different points. A changing mole or a spot that won't heal needs a proper medical skin check, not a cosmetic consultation. On the other hand, sun-related pigmentation, dullness or early signs of ageing skin are appearance concerns that a cosmetic clinic is set up to address, though a skin check is still worth having first if there's any doubt about what a mark actually is.

## Why the distinction matters

Confusing the two can genuinely cause problems. Cosmetic treatments aimed at fading pigmentation or refreshing skin texture aren't designed to diagnose or rule out skin cancer, and a dermatology-focused practice isn't necessarily set up to offer the ongoing cosmetic treatments people want for sun damage, redness or ageing skin. If a spot on the skin is new, changing, bleeding or won't heal, that needs proper medical assessment before anything cosmetic is considered.

## Getting the right starting point

For genuine appearance concerns, a cosmetic skin consultation can help identify what's actually driving the issue, whether that's UV exposure, hormonal pigmentation, or general ageing, and what realistic options exist, since not every concern responds to the same treatment or timeline. For anything that looks medically suspicious, seeing a GP or dermatologist for a proper skin check comes first. Neither a shop-bought product nor a single cosmetic treatment replaces that medical assessment, and no cosmetic clinic can guarantee how skin will respond, since results vary from person to person. If unsure which type of care is needed, it's reasonable to start with a straightforward conversation about the concern itself, rather than guessing which category it falls into.
