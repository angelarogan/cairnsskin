Broadband light, usually shortened to BBL, is a light-based treatment
sometimes used for sun-related pigmentation and redness. Terminology and
exact protocols vary between clinics and platforms, so treatment details
should always be confirmed with the clinic offering it.

## What BBL is generally used for

BBL delivers broad-spectrum light that is absorbed by pigment and by small
blood vessels near the skin's surface. This can make sun spots, freckling and
some redness appear lighter or less visible over a course of treatments. It
is a surface-level treatment aimed at improving appearance, not a way of
reversing the underlying cellular UV damage that caused the pigmentation in
the first place.

## What it does not do

BBL does not prevent new pigmentation from forming, particularly if sun
exposure continues without adequate protection. It is not a substitute for
daily SPF, and skipping sun protection after treatment can mean pigmentation
returns. Results also vary between individuals depending on skin type, the
depth and cause of the pigmentation, and how many sessions are done, so it is
not possible to guarantee a specific outcome in advance.

## Suitability and what to expect

Not everyone is a suitable candidate for BBL. Skin type, recent tan, certain
medications and some medical conditions can all affect whether it is
appropriate, and this needs individual assessment rather than general advice.
A consultation is the appropriate next step to find out whether BBL suits
your skin and what a realistic outcome looks like for your specific
pigmentation.
