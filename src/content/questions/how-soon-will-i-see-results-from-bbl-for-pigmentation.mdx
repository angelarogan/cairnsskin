One of the more confusing parts of BBL for pigmentation is that treated
spots typically look more obvious immediately after a session, before they
start to fade. Knowing this in advance makes the process much less
alarming.

## The first week or two

Immediately after treatment, pigmented spots commonly darken and can
develop a slightly rough, coffee-ground-like texture as the treated
pigment rises to the skin's surface. This is an expected part of the
process rather than a bad sign. Over the following one to two weeks, this
darkened pigment typically flakes away, revealing lighter, more even-toned
skin underneath.

## Why one session usually isn't the full picture

Meaningful improvement in pigmentation generally builds across a course of
several sessions rather than resolving after one treatment, since not
every pigmented cell responds fully to a single pass, and some pigmentation
is deeper than a single session can reach. How many sessions are needed
varies depending on how much pigmentation is present and how it responds
individually.

## What can slow things down

Continued sun exposure without consistent SPF between sessions can
undermine results and allow new pigmentation to form alongside the spots
being treated, particularly in a high-UV climate like Cairns. This is part
of why daily sun protection is emphasised throughout a course of
treatment, not just immediately after each session.

## Setting a realistic expectation

Because timelines and the number of sessions needed vary between
individuals, a consultation is the most reliable way to get a specific
estimate for your own skin, rather than assuming a fixed timeframe that
applies to everyone.
