Rejuran is a polynucleotide-based injectable treatment used to support the
skin's own repair processes and improve overall skin quality and texture.
If you're researching Rejuran in Cairns, understanding what the current
evidence actually supports, what it may help with, the expected downtime,
and who is and isn't a suitable candidate can help you make a more
informed decision before booking a consultation.

## How Rejuran works

Rejuran uses purified polynucleotides, fragments of DNA originally derived
from salmon, delivered into the skin by injection. The proposed mechanism
is that these fragments act as a signal to fibroblasts, the cells
responsible for producing collagen and supporting the skin's structural
matrix, encouraging repair processes rather than physically filling or
resurfacing the skin. This is why Rejuran is generally categorised as a
skin quality or biostimulation treatment rather than a dermal filler or
resurfacing procedure.

## What Rejuran is commonly used for

Rejuran is typically administered as a series of injections, most often
discussed for overall skin quality, including hydration, elasticity and
fine texture, rather than as a treatment for one specific wrinkle, scar or
area of volume loss. It's often considered by people whose main concern is
general dullness or texture rather than a single, targeted issue.

## What does the research say about Rejuran and polynucleotides?

Rejuran itself (REJURAN s) is included on the Australian Register of
Therapeutic Goods (ARTG ID 412632) as a Class III medical device, the
Therapeutic Goods Administration's higher-risk device category, meaning it
has met the TGA's regulatory requirements for safety and quality to be
supplied and used in Australia. That regulatory status reflects safety and
quality assessment rather than a guarantee of specific cosmetic outcomes,
which is why independent research matters just as much when it comes to
understanding what results are actually realistic.

Independent, peer-reviewed research into polynucleotide (PN) and
polydeoxyribonucleotide (PDRN) treatments has grown substantially over the
past few years, though it remains a relatively young field compared to
long-established treatments such as sunscreen or ablative laser
resurfacing.

A 2025 systematic review in the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology examined
nine clinical studies covering 219 patients and found polynucleotide
injections were associated with improvements in wrinkle appearance, skin
texture and elasticity, and were generally well tolerated with mild,
temporary side effects. The same review noted that none of the included
studies achieved a high quality rating on formal appraisal, and called for
more rigorous, standardised research before firm conclusions can be drawn.

A separate systematic review of randomised clinical trials, published in
Cureus in 2026, examined seven RCTs across 183 participants covering skin
rejuvenation, postoperative scar prevention and wound healing, and
reported generally favourable outcomes across these applications, though
the number and size of the trials involved remains modest.

A 2024 systematic review in the European Journal of Plastic Surgery took a
more cautious view, highlighting inconsistencies in study design, dosing
and methodology across the existing polynucleotide literature, and
identified this as a genuine gap that limits how confidently results can
be generalised.

Taken together, the current evidence is best described as promising but
still emerging. Multiple independent studies report measurable benefits
for skin quality with a good safety profile, but study sizes remain
relatively small, methodology varies between trials, and researchers
themselves are calling for more rigorous, higher-quality studies before
strong claims are justified.

## Suitability and limitations

Rejuran is not a substitute for sun protection, and it is not generally
positioned as a treatment for deep acne scarring or significant volume
loss, where other options such as [skin needling](/questions/does-skin-needling-help-acne-scarring/)
may be more relevant to discuss. As with any injectable treatment,
individual suitability depends on skin condition, medical history, any
known fish or seafood allergy, and expectations, which is why this is
genuinely a consultation-first treatment rather than something to
self-select.

## What to expect after treatment, and when results may become noticeable

Some redness, swelling or bruising at injection sites for a few days is
common and generally settles on its own. Because Rejuran is positioned as
a gradual, cumulative treatment rather than an instant one, visible
changes in skin quality, where they occur, tend to build slowly over the
weeks following a session rather than appearing immediately, and may
continue to build across a course of treatments.

## How many sessions are commonly recommended

Rejuran is typically administered as a course of multiple sessions rather
than a single treatment, with the specific number and spacing
recommended varying between individuals based on their skin and goals.
This is worth discussing directly at a consultation rather than assuming
from general information, since an individualised treatment like this
doesn't suit a one-size approach.

## Rejuran in Cairns: what to consider

Cairns has high UV exposure for much of the year, which is relevant
regardless of which in-clinic treatment is being considered. Diligent sun
protection, a broad-spectrum SPF and sensible sun avoidance, matters after
Rejuran the same way it matters after most in-clinic skin treatments, to
protect healing skin and support results.

Cairns' heat and humidity may also be worth factoring into how someone
plans their downtime, since perspiration and prolonged outdoor activity
can feel less comfortable on freshly treated skin, even when clinical
downtime itself is minimal. This is a practical planning consideration,
not a change to how Rejuran works. There is no evidence that climate
affects the treatment's underlying mechanism or effectiveness.

As with any injectable treatment, the aftercare instructions given by your
treating clinician should be followed specifically, since individual
advice may vary based on your skin and the details of your treatment.
Suitability for Rejuran, in Cairns or anywhere else, should always be
assessed individually at a consultation rather than assumed.

## Questions worth asking at a consultation

Before deciding whether Rejuran is right for you, it can help to ask a
treating clinician what specifically it may help with in your case, how
many sessions are likely to be recommended and why, what results are
realistically achievable given your skin, what the known side effects and
risks are, and what the aftercare involves. A consultation is also the
appropriate setting to discuss your medical history and confirm
individual suitability.
