LED (light-emitting diode) light therapy uses specific wavelengths of light,
typically red or blue, aimed at supporting skin recovery, calming
inflammation or targeting acne-causing bacteria, depending on the
wavelength used.

## What it's commonly used for

Blue light is generally associated with acne-prone skin, while red light is
more commonly discussed for redness, inflammation and general skin recovery,
including after other treatments. It is frequently offered as an add-on
alongside treatments such as skin needling or facials rather than as a
stand-alone service.

## What it doesn't do

LED therapy is a gentler, cumulative treatment rather than a dramatic or
fast-acting one, and it is not a substitute for medical treatment of
persistent or severe acne or rosacea. Independent research on specific
outcomes is still developing compared to more established treatments such as
laser or radiofrequency-based options.

## What to expect

Because it's non-invasive with no downtime, LED therapy is low-risk to try
as part of a broader treatment plan, though expectations should stay
realistic: it is generally supportive rather than a primary treatment for
significant skin concerns.
