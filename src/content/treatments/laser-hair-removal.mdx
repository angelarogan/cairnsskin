Laser hair removal uses light energy to target melanin (pigment) in hair
follicles, damaging the follicle enough to slow or reduce future hair
growth. It is one of the most established and widely offered cosmetic laser
treatments.

## How it works

The laser targets pigment in the hair follicle specifically, which is why
hair colour and skin tone both affect how well it works. Darker, coarser
hair against lighter skin generally responds most predictably, while very
light, fine or grey hair can be harder to treat effectively, since there is
less pigment for the laser to target. See
[Laser Hair Removal treatment information](https://www.laserclinics.com.au/laser-hair-removal/)
from Laser Clinics Australia for more on the specific protocol used.

## Why a course of sessions is needed

Hair grows in cycles, and laser is most effective on hair in an active
growth phase. Because not all hairs are in that phase at the same time, a
series of sessions spaced several weeks apart is needed to treat hair as it
cycles through, rather than a single visit.

## Why recent sun exposure matters

Tanned skin contains more surface melanin, which increases the risk of
burns and pigmentation changes during treatment, since the laser can be
absorbed by the surrounding skin as well as the follicle. In a consistently
high-UV climate like Cairns, this is checked at each appointment, not just
the first consultation, since a tan can change between sessions. Treatment
consultations are available at
[Laser Clinics Cairns Central](https://www.laserclinics.com.au/skin-care-clinic/cairns-central/).
