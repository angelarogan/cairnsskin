Skin needling, also called collagen induction therapy, uses fine needles to
create controlled micro-injuries in the skin, prompting the body to produce
new collagen and elastin as part of normal wound healing.

## What it's commonly used for

Skin needling is most often discussed for atrophic (indented) acne
scarring, general skin texture and fine lines. Collagen remodelling can
soften the edges of scars and smooth overall texture over time, though this
is generally described as a softening effect rather than complete removal
of scarring.

## What it doesn't do

Not all scar types respond equally well. Deep, narrow "ice pick" scars and
raised (hypertrophic or keloid) scars typically respond less predictably to
needling alone. It is not a substitute for sun protection, and existing
pigmentation or redness can still be affected by ongoing UV exposure
regardless of needling treatment.

## What to expect

A course of sessions is generally needed rather than a single treatment,
with some redness and sensitivity afterwards being common. Because outcomes
depend heavily on the specific scarring or concern involved, an in-person
assessment is the most reliable way to know whether skin needling suits
your skin.
