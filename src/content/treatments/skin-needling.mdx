Skin needling, also called microneedling, uses fine needles to create
controlled microscopic injuries in the skin. This triggers the skin's
natural wound-healing response, including collagen remodelling, which is
the mechanism behind any improvement in texture or scarring. It's commonly
considered for acne scarring, uneven skin texture, enlarged pores, fine
lines, and some forms of pigmentation or uneven tone, though evidence
suggests results vary by concern and individual, and it isn't suitable for
every type of pigmentation.

## How does skin needling work?

A skin needling device creates hundreds of tiny, controlled punctures in
the skin's surface. This micro-injury triggers the body's natural
wound-healing cascade, inflammation, then new collagen and elastin
production as the skin repairs itself, a process known as collagen
remodelling. Because the channels created are microscopic, the skin's
surface generally heals quickly, while the deeper collagen-building
response continues over the following weeks.

## What is skin needling commonly used for?

Skin needling is most often discussed for atrophic (indented) acne
scarring, general skin texture, enlarged pores and fine lines. It may also
be considered for some forms of pigmentation or uneven tone, though this
needs individual assessment, since not every type of pigmentation responds
the same way, or should be treated with needling at all.

## Does skin needling actually work?

Evidence suggests skin needling can genuinely improve the appearance of
some types of atrophic acne scarring. A meta-analysis of twelve randomised
controlled trials found a statistically significant improvement in scar
appearance from microneedling used alone, with no cases of secondary
scarring or infection reported across the included studies. Results are
not guaranteed, and how well it works depends heavily on the specific scar
type, skin type and how consistently a treatment course is followed. The
section below goes into the research in more detail.

## What does the research say about skin needling?

The Australasian College of Dermatologists lists skin needling among the
recognised treatment options for acne scarring, alongside approaches such
as laser treatment, radiofrequency and surgical techniques.

A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis in Aesthetic Plastic Surgery
pooled data from twelve randomised controlled trials covering 414
participants comparing microneedling with other acne scar treatments.
Microneedling used on its own, without added radiofrequency, achieved a
statistically significant improvement in objective scar appearance. No
cases of secondary scarring or infection were reported in the studies
reviewed, and microneedling was not associated with post-inflammatory
hyperpigmentation, a genuine advantage given darker or acne-prone skin can
be more prone to this with some other treatments. The review's authors
rated this a Level III standard of evidence, a genuine and credible
evidence base, though not yet at the very highest tier of medical evidence
such as large-scale, multi-site trials.

The Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA), Australia's medical device
regulator, also publishes consumer guidance on microneedling specifically.
The TGA notes that microneedling carries real risks, including scarring
and infection, particularly when performed without proper hygiene or
supervision by a health professional, and that a microneedling device or
procedure making a claim to treat a skin condition such as acne scarring
must be included on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG).
This regulatory framework exists because outcomes and safety depend
heavily on who performs the treatment and with what equipment.

Overall, the current evidence for skin needling in acne scarring is
genuinely encouraging with a reasonable safety profile, but it remains a
developing area of research, and individual results vary.

## Skin needling for acne scarring

Skin needling has clinical evidence supporting improvement in atrophic
acne scarring specifically, through the collagen remodelling process
described above. How much a specific person's scarring improves depends on
several factors: the type and depth of scarring present, skin type, the
specific treatment parameters used, how many sessions are completed, and
individual healing response. This is why the same treatment can produce
noticeably different results for two different people, and why an
in-person assessment matters more than general information. For a closer
look at how this plays out across different scar types, see
[does skin needling help acne scarring?](/questions/does-skin-needling-help-acne-scarring/)

## What types of acne scars respond best to skin needling?

Acne scarring isn't one uniform thing, and response to skin needling
varies considerably by scar type and depth.

Rolling scars, broad, shallow depressions with sloping edges caused by
fibrous bands pulling the skin down from underneath, and shallow boxcar
scars, broad depressions with more defined edges, generally respond better
to skin needling than deeper scar types, since the collagen remodelling
process can meaningfully soften these more superficial textural changes.

Deep boxcar scars and ice-pick scars, narrow, deep scars extending further
into the skin, generally respond less predictably to needling alone.
These deeper or narrower scar types may need assessment for a different
approach entirely, such as [fractional radiofrequency](/treatments/fractional-rf/)
or [Rejuran](/treatments/rejuran/), or a combination of treatments, rather
than skin needling as a stand-alone solution. Most people have a mixture
of scar types on the same area of skin, which is part of why a tailored,
individually assessed plan tends to work better than a single generic
approach.

## Skin needling for skin texture and pores

Beyond acne scarring, skin needling is commonly discussed for general skin
texture and the appearance of enlarged pores. The same collagen
remodelling mechanism that can soften scar edges may also firm the skin
around pore openings and smooth overall surface texture over a course of
sessions. As with scarring, improvement is gradual and builds over time
rather than appearing after a single treatment, and results vary between
individuals.

## Skin needling for pigmentation

Pigmentation is worth treating separately and more cautiously than
scarring or texture. Pigmentation has multiple different causes,
sun-related pigmentation, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation left behind
after acne or injury, and melasma, a hormonally-linked condition, all look
broadly similar but behave very differently and are not all suited to the
same approach.

Skin needling may be discussed as part of a broader plan for some forms of
post-inflammatory pigmentation, but it is not automatically appropriate
for every type of pigmentation, and melasma in particular needs careful
individual assessment before any active treatment, since the wrong
approach can worsen it rather than improve it. If pigmentation is your
main concern rather than scarring or texture, that's genuinely worth
discussing directly at a consultation rather than assuming skin needling
is the right starting point.

## How many skin needling treatments may be needed?

Skin needling is typically delivered as a course of multiple sessions
rather than a single treatment, since collagen remodelling is a gradual,
cumulative process. The specific number of sessions recommended varies
between individuals depending on the concern being treated, its severity,
and how skin responds, which is why this is discussed individually at a
consultation rather than following a fixed formula.

## When might results become noticeable?

Because skin needling works by stimulating the skin's own collagen
remodelling process, results build gradually over the weeks following a
session rather than appearing immediately, and continue to develop with
each additional treatment in a course. This gradual timeline is a genuine
feature of how the treatment works, not a sign that it isn't working.

## What is the downtime after skin needling?

Redness and sensitivity for a few days after treatment is common, along
with temporary pinpoint marks where the needles were used, which generally
settle within a day or two. Most people resume normal activities quickly,
though sun protection matters more than usual while skin is healing.

## Potential side effects and risks

Common, temporary side effects include redness, sensitivity and pinpoint
marking at the treatment site. Less commonly, skin needling carries a risk
of infection, scarring or changes in pigmentation, particularly if
aftercare instructions aren't followed or if the procedure is performed
without appropriate hygiene and supervision. The Therapeutic Goods
Administration specifically highlights hygiene and appropriate supervision
by a health professional as important factors in reducing these risks.

## Who may not be suitable for skin needling?

Skin needling is not generally suitable for people with active acne,
active skin infection, a history of poor wound healing, or a history of
keloid scarring. It may also not be the right starting point for certain
types of pigmentation, particularly melasma, without a proper assessment
first. As with any skin treatment, individual suitability depends on skin
condition and medical history, and should be confirmed at a consultation
rather than assumed.

## What should I ask during a consultation?

Before deciding whether skin needling is right for you, it can help to
ask a treating clinician what specifically it may help with in your case,
which scar or concern type you actually have, how many sessions are
likely to be recommended, what results are realistically achievable given
your skin, what the known risks and side effects are, and what the
aftercare involves. This is also the right setting to discuss any
relevant medical history and confirm individual suitability.

## Skin needling in Cairns: what to consider

Cairns experiences high UV exposure for much of the year, and sun
protection is particularly important while skin is recovering from any
skin needling session, since freshly treated skin is more vulnerable to
sun-related pigmentation and irritation.

Cairns' heat and humidity may also be worth factoring into how you plan
your immediate post-treatment activities and downtime. Outdoor exercise,
swimming and extended sun exposure may need to be scheduled around
treatment, since sweating and heat can feel less comfortable on freshly
treated skin, even when clinical downtime itself is minimal. This is a
practical planning consideration, not a change to how skin needling
works. There is no evidence that Cairns' humidity makes skin needling more
or less effective.

As with any skin treatment, the specific aftercare instructions provided
by your treating clinician should be followed closely, since individual
advice may vary depending on your skin and the details of your treatment.
