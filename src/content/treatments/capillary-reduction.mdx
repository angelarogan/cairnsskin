Capillary reduction treatments use laser energy to target small, visible
blood vessels near the skin's surface, commonly on the cheeks, nose or
chin, aiming to make them less visible.

## What it's commonly used for

It is generally used for dilated capillaries (sometimes called broken
capillaries) and visible redness, including redness associated with
rosacea. The laser targets the vessel directly, causing it to close off
and fade from view over time.

## What it doesn't do

This is a cosmetic treatment addressing visible vessels, not a cure for the
underlying cause. For rosacea specifically, capillary reduction can improve
the appearance of existing visible vessels, but it does not stop the
condition from causing new flares or new vessels to form. Ongoing trigger
management and sun protection still matter alongside treatment.

## What to expect

A course of sessions is often needed rather than a single treatment, with
some temporary redness in the treated area afterwards being common. Because
outcomes depend on the specific vessels and skin involved, a consultation is
the appropriate way to find out whether treatment suits your skin.
