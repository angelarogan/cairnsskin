It's a common assumption that skin can't be dry in a humid climate, but
rough or dry skin is still common in Cairns, usually for reasons that have
little to do with outside humidity.

## Why skin gets rough or dry despite the humidity

Frequent air-conditioning indoors creates a much drier microclimate than
outside, and constant temperature swings between air-conditioned spaces and
humid outdoor air can stress the skin's moisture barrier. Over-cleansing or
over-exfoliating, often done to manage oiliness or sweat, can strip natural
oils and worsen dryness. Cumulative sun damage also roughens skin texture
over time, independent of hydration levels.

## What tends to help

A gentle, non-stripping cleanser, a moisturiser suited to your skin type, and
avoiding over-exfoliation are common first steps. Occasional professional
exfoliation, such as microdermabrasion or a hydrating facial, can help
remove built-up dead skin and improve how moisturiser absorbs afterwards,
though frequency and suitability depend on individual skin condition.

## When it's worth checking with a professional

Persistent roughness, flaking or tightness that doesn't improve with basic
skincare changes can sometimes indicate an underlying skin condition rather
than simple dryness, and is worth having assessed rather than treated with
trial and error indefinitely.
