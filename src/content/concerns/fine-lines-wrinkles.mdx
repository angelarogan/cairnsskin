Fine lines and wrinkles develop as skin gradually produces less collagen and
elastin, and repeated facial movement creates lines that eventually become
permanent. Both natural ageing and cumulative sun exposure contribute, but
not equally.

## Natural ageing versus sun damage

Some loss of collagen and elastin happens with age regardless of sun
exposure. However, UV exposure is widely regarded as the single biggest
accelerant of visible skin ageing, a process called photoageing. This is why
sun-exposed areas such as the face, neck and hands tend to show more
pronounced lines than skin that's rarely exposed, and why prevention through
sun protection has an outsized effect over a lifetime.

## What actually slows progression

Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen is the single most evidence-supported habit
for slowing new photoageing. Beyond prevention, treatments such as
fractional radiofrequency and skin needling are commonly discussed for
encouraging collagen production and softening the appearance of existing
lines, though improvement is gradual, varies between individuals, and does
not reverse ageing outright.

## Setting realistic expectations

No skincare product or in-clinic treatment stops the ageing process
entirely. A consultation is the most reliable way to understand what's
realistic for your skin, your specific concerns and your budget, rather than
relying on marketing claims about dramatic or guaranteed results.
