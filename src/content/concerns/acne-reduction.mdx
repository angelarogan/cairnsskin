Active acne ranges from occasional whiteheads and blackheads to more
persistent, inflamed or hormonal patterns that don't resolve with basic
skincare alone. Managing it well usually means addressing oil, blocked
pores and inflammation together, rather than any single fix.

## What drives ongoing acne

Excess oil production, blocked pores, acne-causing bacteria and inflammation
all interact, and hormonal changes can make any of these more pronounced at
different life stages. In a hot, humid climate, higher oil and sweat
production can keep this cycle going more consistently than in a cooler
climate, which is part of why acne can feel harder to get on top of in
Cairns.

## Common approaches

Consistent, non-comedogenic skincare, gentle cleansing and avoiding harsh
over-exfoliation are common starting points. In-clinic options such as LED
light therapy are sometimes used alongside skincare to help manage
inflammation and breakouts, though results vary and no single treatment
works the same way for everyone. Persistent or hormonal acne often responds
better to a plan built around the individual than a generic routine.

## When to seek professional input

Acne that is painful, cystic, scarring or not responding to consistent
skincare over several months is worth discussing with a professional,
since prescription-strength options exist outside of what any skincare
routine or in-clinic cosmetic treatment can offer.
