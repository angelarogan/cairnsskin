Acne scarring forms when inflammation from a breakout damages the
collagen that keeps skin smooth, and the skin doesn't fully rebuild it
during healing. Not all acne leaves scarring, and the type that does form
varies a lot between individuals.

## Types of acne scarring

Atrophic (indented) scars are the most common type, including shallow
"boxcar" scars, narrow "ice pick" scars and rolling scars with a wave-like
texture. Less commonly, raised (hypertrophic or keloid) scars can form,
particularly on the jawline, chest or back. The type of scarring present
significantly affects which treatments are likely to help.

## What tends to help

Sun protection is genuinely one of the most useful habits, since UV exposure
can darken scars and slow how much they fade naturally. Treatments such as
skin needling and fractional radiofrequency are commonly discussed for
atrophic scarring, working by stimulating new collagen production, though
improvement is gradual and varies between individuals and scar types. Deeper
or more defined scars generally respond less predictably to any single
treatment and may need a combined approach.

## Why an assessment matters

Because different scar types respond differently to treatment, and outcomes
vary so much between individuals, a proper assessment of scar type and skin
condition is a more reliable starting point than choosing a treatment based
on marketing alone.
