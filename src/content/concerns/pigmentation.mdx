Pigmentation refers to areas of skin that appear darker or more uneven in
tone than the surrounding skin, such as sun spots, freckling, patchy
discolouration or melasma. It is one of the most common skin concerns raised
in a tropical, high-UV climate like Cairns.

## What causes pigmentation

Melanocytes, the skin cells that produce melanin, become more active in
response to triggers such as UV exposure, hormonal changes, inflammation or
skin injury. Sun-related pigmentation is the most common type, but melasma
(a hormonally-linked form of pigmentation) and post-inflammatory
hyperpigmentation, marks left behind after acne or irritation, are also
common and are managed somewhat differently.

## What actually helps

Daily broad-spectrum SPF 50+ is the single most effective habit for
preventing new pigmentation and stopping treated pigmentation from
returning. Gentle, non-irritating skincare limits inflammation-related
pigmentation from building on top of sun-related pigmentation. In-clinic
treatments, including pigmentation-targeted light therapies, are sometimes
used for established pigmentation, though outcomes vary between individuals
and no treatment guarantees complete or permanent clearing, particularly
without ongoing sun protection.

## When to seek professional advice

Not every dark patch is cosmetic pigmentation. Any spot or patch that
changes shape, colour or size, becomes itchy or bleeds, or looks different
from your other pigmentation, should be assessed by a doctor rather than
treated cosmetically, given Australia's high skin cancer rates.
