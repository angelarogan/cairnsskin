Pore size is largely determined by genetics, but pores can look more prominent
depending on how much oil, dead skin and debris builds up around them, and how
much surrounding skin has lost firmness with age or sun exposure.

## What causes pores to look enlarged

Excess oil (sebum) production stretches the pore lining as it exits the skin,
which is why pores often look larger on the nose, forehead and chin, the
oiliest parts of the face. Sun damage breaks down collagen around the pore
opening, reducing its elasticity so it doesn't spring back as easily. Ageing
has a similar effect, alongside a natural decline in skin firmness.

## What actually helps

Pores cannot be permanently shrunk, since their size is structural, but their
appearance can be minimised. Regular gentle exfoliation and oil-control
skincare can reduce the build-up that makes pores look more obvious. Daily
sunscreen helps protect the collagen that keeps the pore rim firm. In-clinic
treatments such as microdermabrasion or hydrating facials are sometimes used
to improve overall texture and refine how pores look, though results vary
and no treatment eliminates pores entirely.

## When it's worth a professional opinion

If enlarged pores are accompanied by persistent breakouts, scarring or
sudden changes in texture, it's worth discussing with a professional rather
than assuming it's purely cosmetic, since underlying acne or sun damage may
need its own management.
