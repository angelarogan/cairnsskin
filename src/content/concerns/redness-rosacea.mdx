Facial redness can be a temporary reaction to heat, exercise or irritation,
or a sign of an ongoing condition such as rosacea. Telling the two apart
matters, since they're managed differently.

## Redness versus rosacea

General redness usually settles once the trigger (heat, alcohol, spicy food,
a new skincare product) passes. Rosacea is a chronic condition that causes
recurring redness, visible blood vessels and sometimes small bumps, and it
tends to flare in response to specific triggers rather than resolving on its
own between episodes.

## Common triggers

Heat, humidity, sun exposure, alcohol, spicy food and certain skincare
ingredients are all widely reported triggers. Cairns' consistently high heat,
humidity and UV levels mean several common triggers are present for most of
the year, which is why flares can feel more constant here than in a
temperate climate with a cooler off-season.

## What tends to help

Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, avoiding known personal triggers, and gentle,
fragrance-free skincare are common starting points. In-clinic treatments
such as capillary reduction or LED light therapy are sometimes used to help
manage visible redness, though rosacea varies significantly between
individuals and no treatment guarantees complete resolution.

## When to see a professional

Because rosacea can be progressive if left unmanaged, and treatment
approaches differ from those for general redness, a professional assessment
is the most reliable way to confirm what's actually happening and build a
suitable management plan.
