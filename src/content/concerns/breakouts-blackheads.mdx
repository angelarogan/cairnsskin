Breakouts and blackheads both start the same way: a pore becomes blocked
with oil, dead skin cells and bacteria. The difference is mainly whether the
pore stays open (a blackhead, which oxidises and darkens) or closes over and
becomes inflamed (a breakout).

## Why they happen

Overactive oil glands, a build-up of dead skin cells that don't shed
properly, and bacteria that thrive in blocked pores are the main
contributors. Humidity and sweat add to this by increasing how much oil sits
on the skin's surface throughout the day, which is part of why breakouts can
feel more constant in a hot, humid climate than a cooler one.

## What tends to help

Regular, gentle cleansing (especially after sweating), non-comedogenic
(oil-free) skincare and sunscreen, and avoiding picking at blackheads, which
can worsen inflammation and scarring, are common first steps. Overwashing or
harsh scrubbing can actually increase oil production as skin tries to
compensate, so gentle and consistent tends to outperform aggressive.

## When to get a professional opinion

Breakouts that are persistent, painful, cystic or leaving marks behind are
worth discussing with a professional rather than managing with shop-bought
products alone, since the right approach depends on what's actually causing
them.
